## What changed, in plain words

**The light path: a flat form's structure without a frontier model** (2026-10-26, Andrew: "if we can create an
accurate blueprint with dummy labels (p5 g1 r5c6) that's great"; "I'm ok with jev at this point"). The finder proposes
fields, Jev screens them, code and Jev make option groups, the table and block finders (SIA) add structure, and fields
are named by position (`p1_g1_r5c6`, `p2_b1_c3_s4`, `p1_o2`, `p1_f12`). On 5 RRC raw forms against their fillable
versions it found 69% of fields; the misses were mostly the finder, which never proposed them. Two causes fixed
in the experiment (not in step2flat): checkbox characters (□) taller than the glyph rule's 20 pt limit, and underscore
lines fused to neighbouring text. With those and Jev's cut at 0.45: 85%. Copies: `fl__light2_*`.

**FFDNet compared and set aside.** The open CommonForms detector (49 MB, runs on the M1 CPU, in `~/.venvs/commonforms`)
also found 85% of the same fields but mostly different ones; between the two, 95%. Andrew has a business reason not to
depend on it: comparison only.

**The benchmark, defined** (Andrew, 2026-10-26/27). Single pages, purely structural: find each field, say its kind
(text, signatures included, or choice, checkbox and radio together), and find its partners: option groups, and grids
and blocks (same row / column, same copy / slot). Names, compound values (date parts, SSN, address) and meaning are
out ("info about those fields is a much much much higher bar"). About 1000 pages with diversity across pages (caps per
form and per publisher; "VA and IRS forms are all very similar"). Maybe a paper ("it won't take weeks"). Open
questions are in `docs/BENCHMARK_JUDGEMENT_QS.md`.

**Collected from the SafeDocs web corpus** (the first harvest; 09-28 only mined its metadata). 48,881 English,
non-government candidates with form fields; the fetcher reads single PDFs out of the corpus zips and records corpus
id, URL and sha256. First 374 forms felt "extremely homemade" (Andrew), so a Jev question on page 1 ("standardized form
from an established organization, or an informal one-off?") screens them; Andrew's rule: score 0.85 or more and 40 or
more fields, government fine. **The business set: 427 forms** (`runs/acquisition/safedocs/business_set.json`),
staged with blueprints built from each form's own widgets (answer key for detection), the publisher's radio groups
(never merged together), code / Jev groups, and the table and block finders, for Andrew's structure-only check.

**The review page, for checking them** (all 2026-10-27, Andrew's words):

- A **"flat-structure benchmark" section** with the 427 forms as `bm__sd_<id>` ("can you load the benchmark pdfs under
  a new section in the review app?"). The 237 first-batch forms that failed the cut stay under "flat forms" (`fl__sd_`).

- **The React page is the default** at `/` ("can you also make the review app the default now"); the old page moved
  to `/old/`.

- **"accept all"** in the header, temporary ("I'm not doing individual validation of fields on these"): every
  unreviewed field accepted at once; it flips the form to validated, so check the Groups, Grids and Blocks tabs first.

- **"hide tabs"** in the header ("sometimes the tabs ... cover important text"): group grab tabs, grid ids, block labels
  and added fields' names hidden; the boxes stay clickable.

- **Click a printed field to add it** in Add / remove ("click inside a small box and it automatically selects that box
  as a field to add"): a click on nothing known reads the printed box around it off the page (open underline blanks
  are not handled).

- **Page keys work right after opening a form** ("when you load a new doc, the keys to switch pages dont work"): the
  form menu and home search kept the keyboard focus, and an open notes panel took it on every new form. The "g" key
  jumping to another form was the same focus bug.

**The block finder prefers the finer repeat.** On [fl__sd_252381](http://localhost:5005/#blocks/fl__sd_252381) pages
3-8 (three wines of 7 fields a page) it found 6 page-sized copies of 20; a field missing from the page pattern kept
the wines from counting as inside it. Now 18 wines of 7. On 161 forms with saved block types, 3 forms change, none for
the worse.

**Stacked duplicate fields collapsed.** Some publishers put two widgets on one printed field; on
[bm__sd_1474805](http://localhost:5005/#blocks/bm__sd_1474805) p2 that kept the three employment-history blocks from
being found. 38 duplicates removed on 9 benchmark forms (the one the saved block types use kept); the employment
history is now found as 3 copies of 11.

**Grids by geometry, tentatively** (Andrew: "we should define grids partially by geometry ... reasonably uniform
spacing"; "consistent across a row/column"; "there doesn't have to be a field in every single location"; "could we
promote the geometric check tentatively? can we get a jev 'is this or is this not a grid' check?"). The Grids tab
showed a table only when the cells' names were numbered, so position-named forms lost their tables to small blocks
([fl__sd_2924580](http://localhost:5005/#grids/fl__sd_2924580) p1's 23 x 6 entry table,
[fl__sd_7919716](http://localhost:5005/#grids/fl__sd_7919716) p1's education table,
[bm__sd_1115475](http://localhost:5005/#grids/bm__sd_1115475)'s unruled sections). Now a grid shows if the names or the
geometry say it is a table: each column's widths and each row's heights consistent, an even pitch, most positions
filled, labels consistent down most columns (one header, or one caption repeated under every row). A geometry-only grid
reads "geometry only, tentative; Jev 0.99" on its card; it may take over only blocks the finder suggested and nobody
confirmed, and only when they cut across its rows. Jev's scores are stored (`runs/hand_review/grid_jev.json`), not asked
when the tab opens.
